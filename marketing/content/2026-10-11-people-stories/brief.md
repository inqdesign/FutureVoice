# Brief — people stories (새 인스타그램 시리즈, series brief)

> A SEPARATE series beside the feature carousels. The carousel format, BRAND.md, CHANNELS.md
> and the templates stay exactly as they are. Where this series needs something those files
> don't allow yet, it is listed under "Needs a founder ruling" — not changed here.
> Copy below is INTENT only; the copywriter writes ko and en natively.

## One message

Everyone learning a language has one thing they want to say in it, to someone — this series
is that person and that sentence.

(ko intent: 외국어를 배우는 사람에겐 저마다 그 말로 꼭 하고 싶은 한마디가 있다 — 이 시리즈는 그 사람과 그 한마디를 들려준다.)

The app is not the hero. nawana appears once, at the end, as the thing that lets the person
hear that sentence in their own voice (if option 2 = yes), and as a small wordmark.

## Series name candidates

| ko | en |
|---|---|
| 내가 배우는 이유 | Why I'm Learning |
| 그 말을 하고 싶어서 | The Thing I Want to Say |
| 언젠가, 내 목소리로 | Someday, in My Voice |

Recommendation: **그 말을 하고 싶어서 / The Thing I Want to Say** — it names the "why" as
a sentence, which is exactly what the climax delivers, and it doesn't read as an ad.

## Who it's for

The same Instagram scroller as the carousels (studies on and off, can build a sentence,
stumbles out loud) — but caught at a different moment: not "what does this app do" but
"why am I even doing this". They stop for a face and a story, recognise their own reason,
and only then notice the wordmark. Honest floor holds: every person featured can already
build a sentence and stumble. No one is shown starting from zero.

## Why now

The feed so far is product (the "나와나로 할 수 있는 것" carousels). A grid of only product
cards reads as an ad account; growth is the priority and people share people, not feature
lists. The differentiator — the fluent self in YOUR voice — lands harder on a real person's
own sentence than on sample data.

## Episode structure (target 40 s, hard range 30–45 s)

| Sec | Beat | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | **Hook** — the "why" line pulled forward, as a question or a half-sentence | close shot: hands, eyes, a place; big subtitle | person's VO, music starts low |
| 3–10 | **Who** — place + daily life, no name | 2–3 b-roll shots of their ordinary day | VO: where they live, what they do |
| 10–22 | **The moment** — the specific scene where the language mattered (a parent-teacher meeting, a grandmother's phone call, a colleague's joke they missed) | the place/object of that scene, the person thinking | VO carries it; music rises slightly |
| 22–30 | **Where they are now** — honest: what's still hard, the stumble | person trying, mid-sentence; no fake fluency | VO, a real stumbling attempt allowed |
| 30–38 | **Climax — OPTION** (see option 2): their one sentence in the native language, then the same voice says it fluently in the target language. On-screen label: 유창한 나 · nawana (never "now they can") | Futureself pill/circle clipped, light palette, beside or over their face | native line in their real voice → fluent-self line; music dips under both |
| (alt 30–38) | **Without climax:** they say the sentence themselves in the target language, as they can today | face, one take | their own voice only |
| 38–42 | **End card** — series name + small nawana wordmark. No price, no "download", no free-minutes line (bio link does that) | ink on #F7F8FA grid | music tail |

Rules for every episode: one person, one reason, one sentence. Subtitles always on (most
watch muted) — native-language subtitles + English line in the en version. No app screen
recording unless the story needs it; if it does, it comes from the person's real app with
nothing private on it.

## Interview question set (ko, for the interviewer — not copy)

Ask all, keep 2–3 answers. Record the whole conversation as VO material.

1. 지금 어디에서, 어떤 하루를 보내고 있어요?
2. 그 언어를 처음 '해야겠다'고 느낀 순간이 언제였어요? 그때 어디에 있었어요?
3. 그 언어로 말을 걸고 싶은 사람이 있어요? 누구예요?
4. 그 사람한테 꼭 하고 싶은 한마디가 있다면, 우리말로 먼저 해 줄래요?
5. 말이 막혔던 순간 중에 아직도 생각나는 장면이 있어요?
6. 그 언어로 말할 때 나는 어떤 사람처럼 느껴져요? 우리말로 말할 때랑 달라요?
7. 지금 제일 어려운 건 뭐예요? 단어, 말하는 속도, 아니면 자신감?
8. 1년 뒤에 그 언어로 하고 있을 장면을 하나만 떠올려 본다면요?
9. (클라이맥스용) 4번의 그 한마디를 한 번만, 천천히 다시 말해 줄래요?
10. 같은 이유로 배우고 있는 누군가에게 해 주고 싶은 말이 있어요?

Q4 and Q9 produce the climax line. Q6 is often the emotional centre. Never lead the answer
toward nawana; if they mention the app on their own, it may stay.

## Shot list (phone only)

- Vertical 4K 30 fps, back camera, lens wiped, exposure locked on the face; 1× and 0.5×
  only (no digital zoom). Natural window light; no ring light look.
- A. Face, mid-close, eye line just off-lens (listening, thinking) — 3 takes, 10 s each
- B. Hands with the object of their reason (a child's school bag, a letter, a work badge,
  a map, a phone with a family call log — nothing readable) — 3 shots
- C. The place: street, kitchen, commute window — 3 wide shots, 5 s each, one with them in it
- D. Walking-in or sitting-down shot for the "who" beat
- E. The stumble: them saying the line in the target language once, unrehearsed, one take
- F. Quiet face hold, 8 s, for under the climax
- G. Room tone, 30 s, phone on the table, nobody speaking
- No children's faces, no house numbers, no workplace names, no screens with names on them.

## Voiceover recording method

- Same phone, Voice Memos, airplane mode. A small soft room (closet, bedroom with curtains),
  phone 20–30 cm from the mouth, slightly below. A worn wired-earphone mic is fine and
  often better.
- Record the interview as conversation (it sounds alive), then ask for two clean reads:
  the hook line, and the climax line in the native language (Q9).
- The fluent-self line is produced from the person's OWN nawana account and voice clone,
  inside the app — never synthesized by us outside it. Exact in-app path: see Risks 1.
- Mix: VO at about −16 LUFS, music 12–18 dB under the voice, dipped further under the
  native → fluent line so the voice switch is the loudest thing in the episode.

## Consent checklist (per person, signed before the shoot)

- [ ] Appearance: face, voice, place, first name or no name (default: no name, a role only)
- [ ] Their story as told, and they review the final cut before posting
- [ ] **Separate** consent for the voice clone audio used in the episode (one line, this
      episode only, not reused anywhere else)
- [ ] Channels: Instagram, Threads, community page, (TikTok if option 4 allows); paid ads
      excluded unless asked again
- [ ] Right to withdraw: we take the post down on request, no reason needed
- [ ] No private app data shown (their talks, notes, scores) unless named item by item
- [ ] Anyone else in frame (family, colleagues) consents too; children never identifiable
- [ ] Raw footage and audio stored outside the repo and never in Supabase Storage; deleted
      on withdrawal

## How it sits next to the carousels (grid + cadence)

- Cadence: **one episode every two weeks**, carousels in between (≈ 2 carousels : 1 story),
  so the grid reads as product, product, person. Weekly once production is smooth.
- Grid cover: a still of the face (shot A or F) with the series name in the pixel display
  face — must work inside the 4:5 grid crop of a 9:16 Reel.
- Same light ground and one blue accent on the cover and end card, so the two series look
  like one account; the story footage itself is natural colour, not graded to the palette.
- Threads: the episode video + 2–3 conversational lines. Community page: not until the page
  supports video entries (ask the founder).

## First episode candidates (archetypes, no names)

1. A parent raising a child abroad, learning the local language to talk to teachers and other
   parents (the founder's own situation — option 1).
2. Someone with a family member who speaks another language — a partner's parents, a
   grandparent abroad — who wants one real conversation with them.
3. An office worker with an English interview or a promotion that needs presentations.
4. A person who moved for work and still feels like a guest in their own city's language.
5. Someone learning Korean or Japanese for a culture they love, wanting to talk, not just
   watch (target ko/ja exist in the app).
6. Someone returning to a language they once spoke and lost (heritage language).

## Options for the founder (recommendation in bold)

1. **Who's first: the founder himself as a pilot, real users from episode 2.** Consent is
   immediate and it tests the whole shoot / voice / edit pipeline on someone who forgives
   mistakes; shown as a role ("독일에서 아이를 키우는 아빠"), no name, no "founder" label.
2. **Include the voice-switch climax** — it is the one beat no other people-series can have
   and it lands the differentiator without a feature pitch; keep it one labelled beat,
   skipped for anyone who declines the voice consent.
3. **Reels, 30–45 s video** — voiceover + music + a moving face is a Reel by nature, and
   Reels reach non-followers; photo-slide carousels can't carry a continuous voiceover well.
4. **Licence-free track mixed into the video** — it survives cross-posting to Threads/TikTok
   and lets us dip the music under the voice switch; Instagram library audio stays in
   Instagram and is limited for business accounts.

## Risks

1. **How the fluent-self line is made in-app is unverified.** It must come from the person's
   own account and clone (e.g. a Speech script read in their voice, or a line from a real
   talk). Check the exact path in the app before promising the climax; never synthesize it
   outside the app.
2. **Accent on a cross-lingual clone.** A clone recorded in the native language can carry an
   off accent in the target language (seen with Korean samples); listen before using, and
   drop the climax rather than ship a voice the person doesn't recognise.
3. **Ability claim.** The fluent line must never read as the person's current ability or a
   result ("3개월 만에"). Label it 유창한 나 every time; no before/after framing.
4. **Honest floor.** Don't feature anyone who can't yet build a sentence.
5. **Founder's German level** (if option 1 = founder): check he's past the floor in German,
   or the episode breaks rule 4.
6. **Fluent self tone**: the target-language line is the person's own sentence, so it can
   be warm — but no longing lines written for them ("보고 싶었어").
7. **Children / third parties** in the founder's or a parent's episode: no faces, no school
   names.

## Success signal

Per episode, 7 days after posting: average watch time and completion rate (Reel insights),
shares and saves (people stories should out-share carousels), profile visits, comments that
tell their own reason (the series working as a prompt), and DMs volunteering to be featured
(the pipeline for episode 3+). Compare against the carousel posts of the same fortnight.

## Founder decision (2026-10-11)
Format = **Reels**, and the two formats have different jobs: **carousels = the app is the hero** (existing format, unchanged); **Reels = the person is the hero** (this series). A Reel never explains a feature; a carousel never carries a person's story.
